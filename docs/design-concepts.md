# SwimFrancisco Design Concepts

## Concept 1: "The Water Line"

The page has a literal animated water line that sits at a height driven by real SF tide data. Above the water: open water spots with live conditions. Below the water: pools (they're indoors, get it?). The water line gently oscillates. The entire page background color-shifts based on current bay water temperature — deep navy at 50°F, bright teal at 62°F. You feel the water before you read anything.

## Concept 2: "Lane Board"

Modeled after the lane assignment board you see on the pool deck. The homepage is a giant grid — rows are spots, columns are time slots. Green = open, grey = closed. It looks like you walked up to the pool and checked the whiteboard. Monospaced font, slightly off-white background, felt-marker energy. The header just says **"SWIM SF"** in block letters like it was stenciled on concrete.

## Concept 3: "The Big Number"

The hero is one massive number: **"7 places to swim right now."** Below it, those 7 spots listed plainly. As pools close throughout the day, the number ticks down. Open water spots show water temp as a second big number. That's it. No chrome. The URL *is* the brand.

## Concept 4: "Swim Log / Departure Board" (Selected for v1)

Airport departure board aesthetic. Each row is a spot. Columns: name, status (OPEN / CLOSED / LAP SWIM), next session, water temp. Rows flip with that mechanical split-flap animation when data changes. Yellow-on-black or white-on-dark-blue. Feels urgent and real-time even when it's not. A single toggle switches between "NOW" and "THIS WEEK."

## Concept 5: "Postcard from the Bay"

Each spot gets a vintage postcard-style card with an illustrated header (line drawing of Aquatic Park, a pool lane, etc.). Cards are laid out in a grid. Open spots have vivid color, closed ones are faded/greyed. The vibe is "SF Parks & Rec meets Draplin Design" — warm, civic, a little retro. The site feels like a love letter to SF swimming culture, not just a database.
